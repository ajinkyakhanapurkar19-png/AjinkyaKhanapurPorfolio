01 / The project

More than just another messaging application.

Chat PF explores a communication layer for Office OS: a place where conversations sit alongside the tools, documents and decisions that give them context.

A familiar entry point.
A wider workspace.

The mobile challenge is one of structure: make conversations easy to reach while keeping a growing set of work tools understandable on a small screen. The design brings chat, shared content and contextual updates into the same environment.

Bringing structure to connected workflows.

The navigation separates everyday communication from workspace tools. Within chat, channels, direct messages and message-level actions provide progressively more context.

04 / Attention, on purpose

Making room for focused work.

05 / The connected workspace

Moving beyond conversations.

The mobile designs bring selected Office OS updates into chat. Content reviews, meeting context and employee requests each retain a clear source and a relevant next action.

Contextual assistance

An answer with a way back to its source.

The Ask screen places an answer alongside meeting references and a jump-back action. The design makes the supporting context visible instead of leaving the response as an isolated piece of text.

Shown as interface design. Live AI accuracy, automation and integration behaviour are not evaluated in this case study.

06 / Exploration & refinement

From exploration to a more structured mobile experience.

Three source layouts show a visible change in the way the workspace is framed. The comparison focuses on what changed on screen; it does not attribute those changes to unverified testing or feedback.

What the comparison reveals.

The strongest visible shift is in the container system: from a framed workspace to a flatter mobile surface. My takeaway is to judge visual structure against access and readability, rather than treating a new visual treatment as proof of better usability.

07 / Reflection

Designing beyond individual screens.

Chat PF makes me think about mobile design as a connected system: where a conversation begins, how its context travels, and what a person can choose to keep out of view.

The balance is between access and density. Bringing desktop-oriented tools onto a phone means deciding what stays visible, what opens when needed, and how someone finds their way back.
